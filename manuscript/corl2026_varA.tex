%% ── ABSTRACT ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have achieved impressive in-distribution
performance on tabletop manipulation benchmarks, yet they fail sharply when
the spatial configuration of the scene changes at test time---a table height
shift, a repositioned robot base, or a translated object placement can reduce
success rates from near-perfect to near-zero.
We identify the root cause as \emph{coordinate-frame dependence}: all current
VLA action representations, whether discrete bins, FAST-compressed tokens, or
continuous regression heads, are functions of absolute world coordinates and
therefore collapse under rigid-body transforms.
We present \dhb{}, a framework for \emph{invariant trajectory encoding} on
$\se{3}$.
\dhb{} encodes end-effector trajectories into a representation that is
provably invariant to any rigid-body transform, then recovers executable
joint commands via a 7\,ms geometric adaptation step.
On the LIBERO-PRO spatial generalization benchmark, \dhb{} maintains
$\geq$\,X\% success rate under 10\,cm perturbations where FAST and raw-action
baselines drop to $\leq$\,Y\%.
Our simulated Franka Panda proof-of-concept confirms the full pipeline:
encode $\to$ adapt ($\leq$\,7\,ms) $\to$ IK $\to$ MuJoCo execute.
\end{abstract}
