%% ── ABSTRACT ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Every modern Vision-Language-Action model is, by construction,
\emph{coordinate-frame dependent}: actions are represented as absolute
end-effector poses or joint angles, making policies brittle to any
rigid-body change in workspace configuration.
We argue this is a fundamental design flaw, not an empirical limitation,
and propose \emph{invariant manipulation learning} as a corrective principle.
\dhb{} builds this principle into the action representation: trajectories are
encoded on the $\se{3}$ quotient space of shape-modulo-pose, producing tokens
with a provable invariance guarantee.
A 7\,ms $\fatrop{}$-based adaptation step re-grounds the invariant shape to
any target pose, enabling zero-shot spatial generalization without retraining.
We validate across four contributions in priority order:
(1) spatial generalization on LIBERO-PRO and RoboCasa;
(2) geometry-aware action tokenization (41$\times$ token stability, 8 tokens);
(3) sample efficiency via invariant motion retrieval; and
(4) cross-embodiment transfer via embodik IK decoding.
Our results position invariant representations as a foundational building
block for robust, deployable manipulation policies.
\end{abstract}
