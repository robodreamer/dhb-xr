Mathematical Foundations

The geometry behind SE(3)-invariant trajectory encoding

The SE(3)-invariance of DHB-XR is not an empirical finding — it is a mathematical guarantee that follows from the geometry of rotation-minimizing frames on space curves. Just as a curve in 3D is uniquely determined (up to rigid motion) by its curvature and torsion — the fundamental theorem of space curves — a trajectory encoded as DHB invariants captures the shape of motion independently of where it is executed.

The full mathematical treatment covers: Householder double-reflection frame transport, formal SE(3)-invariance and invertibility proofs, the quaternion variant (DHB-QR) that avoids gimbal lock, arc-length reparameterization for speed invariance, and the constrained optimization formulation for geometric adaptation.

Motion Database

Encode once, retrieve anywhere — workspace-blind trajectory search

The problem: geometric redundancy

Large demonstration datasets contain massive geometric redundancy. The same reaching motion recorded at 1,000 workspace positions generates 1,000 database entries — but only ~5–10 unique motion shapes exist. Position-based retrieval degrades as soon as the workspace shifts, because every trajectory's coordinates change even though the underlying motion is identical.

The solution: index by shape, not position

DHB invariants make workspace position invisible. 500 demonstrations (10 tasks × 50 each) are encoded in 19.5 seconds. The resulting index supports retrieval from any workspace configuration — same accuracy at 0 cm offset or 10 cm offset. Adaptation back to Cartesian space takes ~5–10 ms per trajectory via SE(3) decode (unconstrained reconstruction).

Position wins locally; DHB wins everywhere. Position-based retrieval achieves near-perfect same-task accuracy (99.2%) in the training workspace, but cannot find the same trajectory after a 10 cm workspace shift (self-retrieval drops from 100% to 62%). DHB magnitude encoding achieves 63% same-task retrieval — high enough to discriminate LIBERO pick-and-place tasks using only 2 invariants — and 100% self-retrieval at every offset. For multi-workspace deployment, invariant indexing is the right choice.

Beyond tokenization: DHB-XR is not just a tokenizer — it is infrastructure for scalable robot learning. The same invariant representation that gives VLAs stable tokens also enables workspace-blind retrieval, geometric deduplication, and zero-cost spatial augmentation. The augmentation pipeline and the motion database are two applications of the same mathematical core: SE(3)-invariant differential geometry.

Encode is one-time per demo. Unconstrained decode is ~5–10 ms; constrained Fatrop NLP solve is <1 s. Per-step IK at batch size 1 on CPU.

GPU-accelerated batch adaptation (CuSADi): For RL training with thousands of parallel environments, the DHB decoder is compiled to CUDA kernels via CuSADi. Preliminary benchmarks on RTX A2000 (CasADi compiled C backend): 43× speedup at batch 100, 199× at batch 1000, 387× at batch 2000 (1.8 ms for 2000 50-step trajectories vs 685 ms CPU). This enables real-time invariant-space trajectory adaptation across IsaacGym/IsaacLab environments during RL policy training. Comprehensive benchmarks with GPU profiling are in preparation.

Core intuition: the DHB action head is not trying to directly guess the next absolute action chunk. It predicts a structured motion description first, then decodes that description relative to the current end-effector state. Conceptually, it answers: what motion pattern should happen next? and how should that pattern be realized from where the robot is now?
The important result from the SmolVLA phase is not that the action head solved spatial generalization outright. It is that the DHBActionHead is now wired correctly: the current robot state is forwarded, the old rotation-runaway failure is gone, and the decoded trajectory executes cleanly through embodik. The remaining limitation is demo-specific placement robustness, not a broken DHB representation or IK layer.

  Architecture Contribution

  • DHBActionHead is functionally validated as a structured, state-aware decoder rather than a plain open-loop action regressor.
  • The execution stack is sound: embodik stays near 0.47 mm mean FK error across smoke, 50-epoch, and 200-epoch checkpoints.
  • The bottleneck is upstream policy quality, not IK: failure cases are demo-specific placement-margin misses, not decoding collapse.
  • Recommended next step: carry the validated DHB action-head design into stronger backbones such as StarVLA rather than simply training SmolVLA longer.
